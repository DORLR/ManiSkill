## 2026-11-09: why pitch, and the decision to train anyway

* Learning-free geometry (`scripts/analysis/check_pitch_geometry.py`, `results/v3g_pitch_geometry.json`):
  estimating the misalignment by ICP of the frame's plug points to another grasp's inserted plug points
  gave ~0 correlation for pitch *and* yaw -- the method itself fails (yaw is readable by the networks),
  so it proves nothing about pitch.
* Control (`scripts/analysis/check_plug_axis_icp.py`, `results/v3g_plug_axis_icp.json`): can the plug's
  depth points fix its axis? ICP between two grasps of one shape, true axes known: median axis error
  1.1 deg when the grasps differ by < 5 deg, 5.2 deg for 5-15 deg, 13 deg beyond, with 0.3-1.7 mm
  residuals. The visible plug depth (~28 % of plug pixels, mostly pins) does not constrain the plug's
  orientation to the 0.4 deg needed.
* Why pitch only: the misalignment is (plug axis) - (hole axis), both in the camera frame. The hole is
  measured well (board fully in depth). The plug's orientation in the camera depends only on the grasp,
  and the grasp varies only about the pitch axis: for roll / yaw the plug side is a constant the network
  can memorise; for pitch it must be measured from the plug, which is mostly closer than the D405's
  7 cm minimum range (no depth) and small (0.4 deg over a 10 mm pin ~ 0.07 mm, below one pixel at 5 cm).
* User: adding a camera or measuring the grasp first is not acceptable (it would sidestep the
  generalisation to be solved); the world model's commanded-vs-measured hand poses are meant to sense
  jams. Decision: train the world models and the latent FM on the v3g latents without the pitch gate
  (`scripts/pipelines/pipeline_v3g_train.sh`, started 00:01, tmux `qingxin-v3g-train`).
* Contact signal check (`scripts/analysis/check_contact_signal.py`, `results/v3g_contact_signal.json`):
  the offset is regressable from hand poses during jammed pushes (pitch R^2 0.87, also 0.86 under
  pitch extrapolation) -- **but this is leakage**: the commanded motion alone, 0.4 s into the push
  (before contact), gives pitch R^2 0.82. The expert pushes along the true plug axis, so its commands
  carry the truth. The pure contact part (command - measured) gives 0.52 overall but 0.39 already before
  contact, so it is contaminated too. Not answerable from this data.
* Risk for the world model (to verify after training): its condition contains the commanded path, and in
  the training data the commands carry the true misalignment; it may read the misalignment from the
  commands instead of the images. With policy / planner commands (no truth) that shortcut disappears --
  exactly where RL in imagination would exploit it. Check: same contact scene with a truth-free command
  (straight along the hand's nominal axis); does the prediction follow the command or the image? If it
  follows the command: collect contact trials whose push direction does not depend on the true offset.
* Fine-tuning (the supervisor: RL fine-tuning is a must after IL / offline RL): the plan is RL fine-tuning
  of the FM policy inside the world model (imagined rollouts from real observations, reward = progress read
  from the latent, uncertainty penalty), with the simulator only as the referee; an RL fine-tune in the
  simulator as the reference.
* Speed: with the per-pixel pose loss and a fully used GPU 1, CDiT-S takes ~2.1 s/step, CDiT-B ~4.0 s/step
  (300k steps: ~7 and ~14 days). Options discussed (precompute target poses; train S first; fewer steps);
  the user chose to keep everything running as it is for now and revisit later.
