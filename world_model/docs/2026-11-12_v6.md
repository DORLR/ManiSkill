# v6 (2026-11-12, afternoon and evening): plain encoder, joint training, contact state

Times are Toronto. Follows docs/2026-11-12_v5.md. Everything here was decided with the user during the day; the
order below is the order in which it happened, including what turned out wrong.

## Why v6

The user stopped the autoencoder line at 14:24 ("no more patches piled on the structure"): v5, v5b had failed their
gates and v5c was not started. The day's own measurement was the argument: a plain ResNet-18 trained directly read
the pin tip's offset to 0.22 mm where the UNet -> crops -> autoencoder pipeline gave 0.39-0.77 mm.

Decided with the user:

* No reconstruction, no UNet, no crops, no decoder. One plain ResNet on the whole 320 x 320 RGB-D image; its feature
  map is what the world model predicts and what every policy reads (FM, Diffusion Policy, BC share it).
* The joint adapter is deleted. The world model itself gives where the hand goes.
* The success classifier becomes an output of the world model ("inserted" at the target time): the judge of imagined
  outcomes. Not "inserted within 8 s" as a condition (that would fix the outcome whatever the actions).
* Encoder and world model are trained together from scratch (the user's choice over train-then-freeze).
* Data stay scripted (demonstrations, exploration, the contact set): the experiment is one policy-independent world
  model improving several algorithms on unseen shapes and grasps, so no policy rollouts in its training data.
  Held out as before: ports A-D (test, untouched), 13 generated shapes and all out-of-band grasps (ood_val).
* Label for the encoder: the remaining motion to the inserted pose by FK of the grasp's one insertion (real-robot
  obtainable when the episode contains one insertion; not available and not needed at use time) and "inserted now".
  Checked on 233 held-out trajectories: against the true offset from the hole centre it has a constant bias
  (0.13 mm low, the plug rests low in the clearance) and 0.03 mm (median) / 0.06 mm (90 %) of scatter.
* CDiT unchanged; 6.2 s history, vision masking, random time shifts, context noise as in v4.

Read: the 20 x 20 x 16 map loses nothing (B vs A); a larger map or a larger ResNet does not help; the whole image
with the standard stem loses most against the crops, and leaving out the max-pooling with trained batch-norm
recovers all of it (F = the crop reader); the FK label as global point motions loses another part, the per-cell
form recovers most of it. D (true tip target on all three sources) learned nothing and is not interpreted; H and I
were stopped. **The user then set the encoder's precision aside** ("vision reading it is not the point; test the world
model"): others do tight insertion by contact reaction, not by reading 0.2 mm; yesterday's own decomposition showed
the damage came from the hand under contact (37 % with the real hand motion, 89-96 % with the imagined one, 25.5 % in
the simulator). F's stem and J's label are measured and on record, not yet used in the joint runs.

No collapse in any run (feature std over samples 0.6-0.7). About 0.5-0.9 s per step depending on what else runs.

### World model at 10 000 steps (`joint_v6_res1`), held-out contact set, replay of recorded commands for 12 s

* Hand along the axis, imagined minus real: median 0.13 / 0.12 / 0.23 / 0.27 mm after 2 / 4 / 8 / 12 s, no drift.
* Decisions in which the real hand stayed although the command led (3175, lead 0.63 mm on average): imagined advance
  0.01 mm per decision (v4e's adapter: 0.36-0.38). Exploration set: 0.08.
* "Inserted": really 25.0 % / 29.2 % after 8 / 12 s, called 1.0 % / 2.6 %, AUC 0.94 -- the ranking is right, the level
  far too low (v4e called 89-96 %).
* Read-out across the axis from the imagined features 1.18 -> 1.72 mm (2 -> 12 s), from the real frame's features
  0.76 -> 0.58, blind 1.03 -> 0.74: the predicted features are worse than blind.

### Closed loop: the script pushes 0.6 mm ahead of the imagined hand (128 pressed held-out states, 16 s)

| run, step | imagined hand moved, median (mm) after 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 s | per decision |
|---|---|---|
| `joint_v6_res1`, 10 000 | 1.0 / 2.9 / 6.7 / 14.5 | 0.37 |
| `joint_v6b`, 5 000 | 0.7 / 2.0 / 5.0 / 11.2 | 0.29 |
| simulator (by selection of the states) | under 0.3 | |

So with recorded commands the model keeps the hand, with a script's fresh commands it does not. Three explanations of
mine were tested and are wrong or incomplete:

1. the commanded orientation (taken from the imagined hand or kept): identical results;
2. the 9th path command, which in recorded data is decided after the hand was seen: no difference;
3. the hand output written as "measured minus commanded" (so that a held hand is "-lead", and the model learns "the
   lag persists"): side by side on the same states the moved amount was about the new lead minus the lead the state
   had seen (0.37 for 0.6 vs 0.17-0.26; 0.15 for 0.6 vs 0.41; 0.09 for 0.6 vs 0.57), but not for states that had seen
   a larger lead, and rewriting the output as the hand's own motion (`joint_v6b`) changed the creep only from
   0.37 to 0.29 mm per decision.

What is established: the model has no persistent state "pressed against something"; it decides it anew at every step
from what commands and history look like, and one wrong decision makes the history look free. One-step validation on
the data's own pressed decisions is fine in every run (+0.03 mm at step 5000 of `joint_v6b`).

### v6c (the user's proposal, running)

The contact state now -- `pressed` (proprioception only: over the last 0.4 s the hand did not move along the axis,
< 0.05 mm, although the last command was ahead of it, > 0.1 mm) and `inserted` (contact switch) -- is a condition of
the world model, and `pressed` at the target time a third extra output. In imagination the outputs of a step are the
state of the next. It is the present state, not the future outcome. Checks at 5000 / 10 000 / 20 000 / 30 000 steps:
closed-loop push, replay on the contact set and on exploration (`logs/pipeline_v4.log`, `runs/checks_v6/`).

## Open

* The predicted features are poor (worse than "nothing changes" one step ahead, worse than blind over seconds).
* "Inserted" is called far too rarely in imagination.
* Which property of a fresh command makes the model let go of the hand is not found.
* The measured encoder gains (no max-pooling, trained batch-norm, per-cell label) are not in the joint runs.
* Action discrimination (push vs slide from the same state) is still untested; no policy has used v6 yet.
